# YC1: signed yaw check and parked share of Part A (2026-11-27)

Offline measurement on the batch's recorded runs `g1x_tour_S01..S12`. No engine code, scorer, grading doc or REGISTERED_TESTS.md was
touched. Script: `2026-11-27-yaw-check.py` (`python3 -I`, about 15 s; it reads the scorer's replay cache, see caveat 1).
Angles are in degrees, errors are estimate minus truth in the scorer's frame (registered conventions).

## Verdict

**Outcome 1, with outcome 2 not excluded.** For the objects that are locked to the staged wall, the signed yaw error is the
direction error of the staged wall edge in the scorer's frame, to within 0.3 degrees; that term carries most of the registered yaw
excess. I cannot say whether that direction error belongs to the published polygon or to the scorer's frame rotation, because
the scorer's rotation is itself the thing the polygon is compared with, and its own uncertainty is as large as the error.

Key numbers (37 graded visits that have a true Webots wall: nightstand 17, bed 11, toilet 9):

- **The yaw term is the wall term.** Writing e_yaw = wall_err + e_rel (wall_err = staged edge direction minus true wall direction, both
  in the published frame; e_rel = the object against the staged wall, which does not depend on the frame fit), the median e_rel is
  +0.01 deg, and 24 of 37 visits have |e_rel| < 0.3 deg. wall_err carries **71 percent of the summed yaw z^2**
  (nightstand 66, bed 86, toilet 79). Regression of e_yaw on wall_err through the origin: slope 1.05.
- **It is the engine's sigma that is too small for it.** Locked objects report sigma_yaw = 0.02 m / L = 0.17 deg (room_3, L 6.6 m) and
  0.35 deg (room_2, L 3.3 m); the staged wall's direction error has rms 0.74 deg and 1.00 deg in those rooms (mean 0.58 deg negative in room_3).
  z of a locked object is therefore 3 to 9 (S04 bed -5.4, nightstands -5.2 and -5.5; S12 nightstand -8.9; S07 -3.3).
- **What the registered statistic does without it.** Mean full-P NEES/DOF with e_yaw replaced by e_rel (wall_err subtracted, same P):
  nightstand 4.51 to 2.61, bed 3.06 to 1.98, toilet 4.09 to 3.13. With the yaw error set to zero (the 6th component of e only, P unchanged):
  1.56, 1.74, 2.85. So removing the wall term brings the bed within reach of the registered [0.5, 2] and leaves the
  nightstand and toilet above it; it does not by itself pass test (a).
- **Common-mode in a tour/room: partly.** Objects on the same staged edge in one tour: 23 pairs, same sign 14/23, correlation of
  e_yaw -0.19, and the group mean carries 44 percent of the squared error. The correlation is low because of three objects that are
  not locked (S03 nightstand_L -14.6 deg, S05 bed -4.9, S10 nightstand_L -6.4). Among the locked ones the agreement is exact
  (S04: bed -0.95, nightstands -0.91 and -0.95, wall_err -0.97).
- **Not the frame fit, as far as it can be tested.** The frame-fit quantities I can build explain little of wall_err: correlation with
  (rotation over the whole run minus registered) +0.47, with the stay's first 5 s +0.44, with the last 5 s -0.28, with a position-based
  rotation -0.20, slopes 0.2 to 0.4 (not 1). Replacing the registered rotation by a whole-run rotation makes wall_err larger
  (rms 0.87 to 1.85 deg heading, 3.18 deg positions: the published frame is not rigid over a run, so the registered per-stay fit is
  the sensible one). The sign is consistent by room: room_3 10 of 11 tours negative, mean -0.58, sd 0.50; room_2 mixed (3/6, mean -0.06, sd 1.05).
  Against that: the heading offset the scorer averages to get its rotation has a standard deviation of 0.84 deg inside a stay
  (median), and the stay's positions span 0.7 m (median), so a position fit is too noisy to overrule it. The data cannot separate
  "the polygon is rotated" from "the stay's mean heading offset is the wrong rotation" below about 1 deg.

What this means for the order (Fable's Q2/Q3): the direction error exists and is the largest single piece of the yaw term. Item 3
(carry the wall direction in the wall latent, sigma read from the room) addresses it IF the direction error is the polygon's. If it is
the scorer's rotation, the cure is the scorer amendment (truth uncertainty added to P), not the engine. One cheap experiment decides
it and is not done here (it needs a frame rotation measured without the robot's heading): see "What would separate them".

## Experiment 1 in detail

Reproduction check: this script's per-class numbers equal the registered file `g1x_tour_nees.txt` (per-DOF yaw 17.39, 7.64, 7.61,
7.24, chair 40383.54; mean NEES/DOF 4.51, 3.06, 4.09, 3.27, 6750.94). 72 full-P visits: nightstand 17, bed 11, toilet 9, refrigerator 4,
hood 1, chair 30. The two table visits use the legacy diagonal log and are left out.

How the wall error is measured. The replay dump's frame record holds the staged wall (`p`, `n`, `length_m`, `edge`) of every update; the
draw's staged edge direction is atan2(n_x, -n_y) in the published frame. The true wall is the Webots `Wall` node whose centre
line is nearest to the staged edge's foot point (taken to the world by the scorer's own `Frames.pub_to_world`), direction = its long
axis. It is taken into the published frame by the scorer's own rotation and gauge, so e_yaw and wall_err share the same frame rotation and
e_rel = e_yaw - wall_err does not depend on it. room_1's walls are meshes: the nearest `Wall` node is 3 m from the staged edge, so
the refrigerator (4) and hood (1) have no true wall direction and are left out of the wall analysis (they stay in the first table).
Chairs are not analysed for yaw here: 11 of the 30 have |e_yaw| above 45 degrees (mode errors).

## Experiment 2 reading

Parked is rare where it would matter: nightstand 24 percent of the graded-visit updates, bed 17, chair 10, toilet 0, refrigerator 55, hood 78
(one visit). Of those, the updates a per-point persistent share (F2) can act on are the REPEATS of a parked view, that is every parked
update after the first of a run: nightstand 23, bed 16, chair 10 percent. Together nightstand and bed: 440 of 2266 updates (19 percent).
The draw itself (the visit's last update) is taken while the robot is moving in all 74 visits, because a stay ends when the robot
leaves. Parked updates are 3 percent (nightstand) and 2 percent (bed) of the last third of a visit, so F2's reach on the draw is small by
construction; for test (a) the parked share bounds F2 at roughly one update in five on the two classes that are still candidates.
LiDAR-only updates are 49 and 74 percent of the nightstand and bed rows and 1 to 3 percent of rows are both parked and LiDAR-only, so
the parked repeats are mostly camera updates.

## What would separate outcomes 1 and 2

The frame rotation of a stay is the mean of the robot's own heading error over it, so a rotation measured from the robot's heading or from
its positions inherits the localisation's drift. An independent rotation needs a measurement that does not use the robot's estimated
heading: for example registering the replayed LiDAR scans of the stay to the world's wall meshes with the Webots pose (the scan tape
`scan_tape_*.bin` is in each run), which gives the published frame's rotation to the order of 0.1 deg. If wall_err survives that, it
is the polygon (item 3); if it vanishes, it is the scorer (amendment). About a day; not done because it needs the scan tape reader and
the world mesh, and the brief ruled out new tooling.

## Caveats

1. **The replay binary in this worktree was rebuilt on 2026-11-27 21:14 (CW1's containment).** The scorer's bit-identity gate now
   rejects every visit with it. The script therefore reads the scorer's own cache of the batch's binary (built 2026-11-26 19:54, HEAD
   9dfbf82d, `--theta-cov`): per dump, the cached run with exit 0 whose rows all have dth_live = dP_live = 0. The reproduction check
   above shows it is the same statistic. A fresh run of `first_visit_nees.py` against the current binary would fail the gate, which is
   correct, and says nothing about this analysis.
2. The true wall direction depends on the Webots `Wall` size convention (thickness x length; the long axis is the wall's line). Verified by the
   result itself: the true-wall distance of the staged edge is 0.06 to 0.15 m for all 37 (thickness / 2 plus the polygon's trace).
3. gt_pose.tsv is sampled about every 1.16 s and rounded to four decimals. "Parked" means both bracketing samples are identical to that
   rounding; a robot that moves and returns within 1.16 s would be counted parked (not seen in the data). Speed columns are given as the
   sensitivity: below 0.005 m/s 37 percent of all rows, below 0.02 41, below 0.05 49.
4. "yaw DOF removed" in the NEES table sets e_yaw to 0 with P unchanged (a conditional, not the 5-DOF marginal).
5. Small n: 20 distinct (tour, room) walls, 9 in room_2 and 11 in room_3; the room_3 sign consistency (10/11) is the strongest single
   statement and is on one wall edge of one room.
6. Measurement only: nothing here is fitted into the engine, and no bound is set from these values.
