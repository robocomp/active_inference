Producer ↔ consumer loop through the affordance node

A concept agent (producer) writes an epistemic affordance onto a shared DSR graph node; the controller (consumer) reads, claims, and executes it. The node is a blackboard: the whole protocol rides on two booleans, active and epistemic_pending. Object-relative viewpoint fields (aff_view_*) let the producer say what view it needs in the object's frame while the controller owns feasibility.

Figure 1. Affordance as a shared-graph contract between an active-inference perception agent and a controller. The producer's next-best-view planner scores candidate faces by the D-optimal expected entropy reduction on its belief covariance, bounded by the adequacy gap to a consumer-declared precision Σ*, and publishes an object-relative viewpoint constraint (ranked faces + per-face gain in nats + stand-off band + framing + Σ*) rather than a world pose. The controller selects among all competing affordances by expected free energy G = λ·nav_dist − epistemic_gain, claims one through the active/pending handshake, resolves the constraint into a collision-free reachable pose (Part B), and executes it via a servo feedback loop bound to the parent object node. As evidence accumulates, the belief covariance contracts, the adequacy gap and thus epistemic_gain fall to zero, and the affordance self-extinguishes — a threshold-free "done" set by the consumer's precision demand.
