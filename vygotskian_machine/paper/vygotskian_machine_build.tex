\begin{abstract}
We propose a robot architecture that follows Vygotsky's account of how higher mental functions develop, first
between people and then within the individual. The robot is built on \cortex{}, an existing architecture in which
Active Inference agents maintain a probabilistic hierarchy of beliefs about the robot, its rooms, the objects in them
and aggregates of objects, and in which a language agent already plans and talks with a person. We add two things.
The first is an inner voice, a loop in which a vision-language model (VLM) proposes questions and tests and only the
robot's perception changes the answers, called on by a demand signal built from the surprise that the agents report.
The second is a cultural plane, in which the VLM supplies a level-4 hypothesis about what each room is for, writes
missions whose steps predict their own belief outcomes, and imagines on the robot's own models while demand is low.
The VLM's knowledge enters as pseudo-counts and stored missions that the robot's own experience progressively
replaces. We relate the proposal to Sutton's bitter lesson and to the argument that intelligence cannot be fully
amortised, and we design seven experiments, not yet run, that would show whether culture rather than the format of
language is what the machine uses.
\end{abstract}
